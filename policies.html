Respect &
wellbeing at the lab

Here are a few recommendations to help us all build a pleasant, respectful and safe working environment, for the members of our team as well as for the visitors and volunteers we welcome at the lab.

1

Let’s Treat Everyone in the Laboratory with Respect

🧍Let’s respect everyone’s physical integrity.
🔒Let’s respect everyone’s privacy and personal boundaries.
🤝Let’s respect everyone’s dignity.
💬Let’s communicate and behave respectfully.

You can find recommendations on the CRNL wiki under:

Services and Cross-Functional Groups→Equality→Our Documents (at the bottom of the page)→Some Tips for Building Positive Workplace Relationships
↗

Discrimination, harassment, and any behavior that undermines a person’s dignity are not tolerated.

2

Let’s welcome volunteers in good conditions

☎️To contact and recruit volunteers, let’s use a work phone and a work email address as much as possible, or even an address dedicated to recruitment.
🧑‍💼When an experiment involves welcoming a volunteer, let’s make sure a colleague is informed, reachable and nearby if needed.

These practices help guarantee an appropriate working and welcoming environment, in the interest of everyone involved.

3

If you face a difficulty, don’t stay alone!

If you are facing an uncomfortable situation, inappropriate behaviour or a relationship difficulty, you can talk to someone you trust. Several people are available to listen, advise and guide you, in a confidential setting.

Within IMPACT
  • Your colleagues.
  • Andréa Auvray, PhD student, for students.
  • Your PI or another PI.
  • Fadila Hadj-Bouziane and Alessandro Farnè, directors of IMPACT.
Within the CRNL
  • The Equity-Diversity-Inclusion group: eid.crnl-lyon@inserm.fr (see the active members on the wiki).
  • Christina Schmidt, deputy director in charge of human resources.
With your supervising institutions (CNRS, INSERM, Université Lyon 1, Université Lyon 2, etc.)
  • The occupational physician.
  • The occupational psychologist.
  • The mediator.
  • The equality and diversity officers or offices.

You will find a lot of information and resources on the CRNL wiki, under:

Services and Cross-Functional Groups→Equality
↗

Asking for advice or reporting a difficulty is legitimate. You don’t have to handle a worrying situation alone.
